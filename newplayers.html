What the game is

The 30-second version, so the rest of the page makes sense.

TWRPG is short for The World RPG. It's a custom map for Warcraft III, not a standalone game — you open Warcraft III, go to Custom Games, and host or join a TWRPG lobby. Up to 10 people play together in one world.

You pick one hero out of 37 and keep it forever. You kill monsters, level up, collect items, craft better items out of the ones you collected, and use those to kill bigger bosses — which drop the parts for even better items. That's the whole loop. There is no ending; the goal is a stronger character.

Because it's a Warcraft III map, the game cannot remember you between sessions. That's what the save code exists for, and it's why the save section below is the longest one on this page.

Your first game

Nothing here is permanent except your hero choice, so don't agonise.

  1. Pick a hero. At the start you choose one of 37 heroes. This is permanent for that character — if you want a different one, you make a new character later. Browse them all first on the Characters page.
  2. Kill things near the starting village. Early areas are level 1–40 and completely safe to learn in. Monsters give experience and gold.
  3. Do the tutorial quests. A fairy follows you around — that's your quest giver. Press Q on it and it tells you what to do next and where to go. The quests hand out experience and a few starter materials, and they walk you through the map.
  4. Spend your stat points. Every level gives points. Put them into your hero's main attribute — see the next section.
  5. Pick up everything. Materials that look like junk are crafting parts for real gear. Check anything in the Item Codex before dropping it.
  6. Type -save myhero before you leave. Then read the saving section so you actually keep this character.
💬

Ask in the lobby. TWRPG is co-op, not competitive. Veterans routinely drag new players through bosses far above their level, which is the fastest way to gear up. Say you're new. The community is on the TWRPG Discord.

Levels, stats and your main stat

Which number to grow, and why the others barely matter.

Levels

Normal max level is 100. Levelling is fast compared to how long gearing takes, so don't stress about it — you will hit 100 long before you have good items.

Past 100, levels are bought, not earned: you trade Soulstones (rare drops from the endgame Arcana bosses) to an NPC at the Mage Tower. Each level from 101 to 110 costs its own specific Soulstones — 110 is the real ceiling. That's deep endgame; ignore it for now.

The three stats

Every hero has Strength (STR), Agility (AGI) and Intelligence (INT), and exactly one of them is its main stat. The main stat is what turns into damage — the other two do very little for you.

  • Put your level-up points into your hero's main stat. That's the whole strategy.
  • A hero's main stat is listed on the Characters page as its primary attribute.
  • Some heroes play differently depending on which stat is highest — for example a few Paladin and Hermit skills change effect entirely on a STR build versus an INT build. The skill descriptions on the Characters page spell out which parts are main-stat specific.
  • Made a mistake? Resetting stat points is free — the NPC for it stands in Capital Prius, next to the Recall Stone. Nothing here is permanent, so experiment.

Saving — the most important thing on this page

What -save actually does, where the file lands, and what's inside it.

What -save does

Type -save in the game chat. Two things happen:

  • The map writes a .txt file onto your computer listing your character and everything it owns.
  • Inside that file are your load codes — long strings of letters and numbers. Those codes are your character. Nothing else is.

Give the file a name so you can find it again:

-save myhero

Without a name the map invents a random filename, which is how people lose characters. Always give it a name.

Where the file goes

On Windows:

C:\Users\<your Windows user>\Documents\Warcraft III\CustomMapData\TWRPG\

On Mac:

~/Library/Application Support/Blizzard/Warcraft III/CustomMapData/TWRPG/

Your saves are the .txt files in that folder. Open one with Notepad or any text editor.

Back this folder up. Copy it to a second drive, a cloud folder, anywhere. A Warcraft III reinstall, a Windows reset, or a disk wipe takes every character you own with it.

When to save

  • After anything good drops. Not "later". Immediately.
  • Before you leave a game — every single time.
  • Every 20–30 minutes in a long session, out of habit.

Saving costs nothing and takes a second. There is no penalty for saving 40 times in one game. There is a very large penalty for saving zero times.

💡

-asave and -aload are your account, not your character. They save and load only the account-bound things — the Grandmaster Icon and its upgrades, and the rest of what belongs to you rather than to one hero. Your character, level and gear still need plain -save / -load; the two pairs are not interchangeable.

One warning straight from the patch notes: don't use -asave to work around gameplay restrictions. It's there to carry your account across games, not to duplicate things.

Loading your character back

Getting into the next game with the character you had in the last one.

The steps

  1. Join a TWRPG game and get to the point where you'd pick a hero.
  2. Open your save .txt from the CustomMapData\TWRPG folder.
  3. Copy Load Code 1 — the whole line including the -load — and paste it into the game chat. Press enter.
  4. Do the same with Load Code 2.
  5. Your hero, level, stats, gold and items all come back.

Order matters in practice: code 1 brings the character, code 2 brings the items. If you only paste code 1 you'll get a hero with an empty inventory.

Why a code sometimes refuses to load

  • Wrong account name. Codes are tied to the name that saved them. Loading on a different Battle.net account or a renamed account will fail. This is deliberate — it stops people from copying each other's characters.
  • Typo or truncation. Copy the entire code. One missing character kills it. Don't retype by hand.
  • Too old. Codes work from map version v0.62s onward. Anything older than that won't load into v0.76a.
  • Very old codes get adjusted. When max level was cut from 420 to 100, old codes started loading at level ÷ 4, and stat points reset so you have to re-spend them. Expect an old character to need a bit of fixing up.
👤

You can read your codes here too. Paste them into My Characters on this site and it decodes them in your browser — class, level, stats and every item. Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Your gear then ticks itself off the farming list in the Build Maker, so you can see what you still need.

Items and gear slots

Five slots. That's the entire equipment system.

Your character wears exactly five things:

SlotWhat it does
WeaponBiggest damage source. Restricted by weapon type — see below.
HeadwearStats, usually defensive or utility.
ArmorSurvivability — armor, HP, damage reduction.
WingsStats plus movement; often has an active effect.
AccessoryThe flexible slot. Rings, orbs, and most of the interesting effects.

Most endgame gear also requires level 100. Lower-grade pieces have lower requirements, so you gear up in steps as you level.

Your hero can only hold certain weapon types. Buying a beautiful staff for a hero that uses guns is a classic first-week mistake.

Specialty items

Some items carry an extra effect only when a specific hero holds them — an upgraded version of one of that hero's skills. These are listed per hero on the Characters page. They're usually a big deal for that hero and worthless to anyone else.

Grades — the power ladder

Every item belongs to a grade. The grade tells you both how strong it is and which bosses you have to beat to build it.

Magic / Rare Starter Early field monsters — King Kong, Troll Lord, Hydra, Blood Wraith. Placeholder gear you replace fast.
Deltirama Early Your first real set. Wings of Death, Mage Lord, Count of Wallachia, Jack o Lantern, Guardian of Sea, Avalon’s angels.
Neptinos Early–mid Corruptor Rectus, Turtle Lord, Demon Lord Beriel, Flame Nightmare, Frostspider Lord/Queen, Soul of Everfrost, Spirit Beast.
Gnosis Mid Skeleton King Desperia, Shadow Dragon Irbert, Zombie Lord, Ancient Ent, Bone Dragon, Archangel Samael.

📈

Read it as a route, not a rating. To wear Gnosis gear you farm Gnosis bosses; to kill Alteia bosses you generally want Gnosis gear first. Each grade is the ticket to the next one. Everything on the Item Codex is colour-coded by grade, and Boss Drops shows which boss drops what, at what rate.

Every boss above is a link. The Alteia and Arcana names open their written guide directly; the rest open their drop table.

Icons

The thing new players ask about most. They aren't gear.

An Icon is a trophy. Killing a particular boss has a small chance to drop that boss's Icon — Turtle Lord Icon, Zombie Lord Icon, Nereid Icon, and so on. Around thirty of them exist, one per notable boss.

Icons do two things:

  • They prove you killed the boss. Collecting them is a long-term goal in itself, and having the right ones is how you get the big combined Icons.
  • Some of them buff your drop rates. This is the important part. The Grandmaster Icon raises personal loot drop rate by 50% for Deltirama through Gnosis. The Legend Icon raises it by 50% across Deltirama through Alteia, and also carries a combat proc. Certain boss Icons (Nereid, Ifrit, Valtora, Agareth, Death Fiend) carry that same proc — roughly a 5% chance on attack or skill cast to trigger Elemental Fury and gain 2% main stat for 30 seconds.

So a Legend Icon is not a decoration. It makes every hour you spend farming meaningfully more productive, which is why it sits behind Soulstones and a pile of other Icons.

The Grandmaster Icon and its upgrades are account-bound, not character-bound — they travel with -asave / -aload rather than your character's save code, and they apply to every character on that account.

A few Icons can also be bought rather than farmed — Gold Coins buy field boss Icons at the coin trader, and there's an NPC in Alfon Village that hands certain Icons to players who already hold the Master Icon.

Tokens

Cosmetics. Absurdly rare cosmetics.

A Token turns you into the boss it came from — a costume, a mount, a transformation. Duke Token, Fiend Token, Soul Token, Token of Darkness, and so on. Nearly every boss in the game has one.

They are the rarest things in TWRPG. Typical drop chance is 0.005% to 0.01% per kill — one in ten to twenty thousand. Nobody farms tokens on purpose; they happen to you.

  • Tokens are pure cosmetic. They don't make you stronger. They are, however, the loudest possible flex in a lobby.
  • The Crystal Golem Tokens (green, blue, red, white, yellow) are the exception — they drop far more often (0.1–2%) and craft together into the Rainbow Crystal Golem Token.
  • A couple are earned rather than dropped, like the Immortal Kitty Token for killing Zombie Lord nine times in one run.
  • Cosmetics you own live in your closet, opened through your storage. They're registered automatically before saving.

Save your replay — Gnosis and above

🎥

Rule: from Gnosis grade upward, if a Token or an Icon drops, save the replay of that game. The map itself prints "!Warning! Please save a replay of this game." on the highest Icons for exactly this reason.

A replay is the only proof the drop ever happened. If the item is lost to a crash, a desync, a bad save, or a bug, the replay is what lets the map's staff verify it and give it back. No replay, no restoration — and at 0.005% you do not get another one.

How to actually keep a replay

  1. Warcraft III writes every finished game to Documents\Warcraft III\Replays\ as LastReplay.w3g.
  2. It is overwritten by your next game. That's the trap — play one more round and your proof is gone.
  3. So the moment the drop happens: finish or leave the game, then go rename or copy LastReplay.w3g to something like soul-token-2026-09-05.w3g. Reforged's end-of-game screen also has a Save Replay button.
  4. Then -save, keep the code, and post in the TWRPG Discord if something went wrong.

Same habit applies to any drop you'd be devastated to lose — Soulstones, Coins of Effort, Arcana pieces. It costs you ten seconds.

Bosses

Three kinds, and they behave very differently.

KindHow it works
Field bosses Stand in the open world and respawn on a timer (a few minutes). Ragnaar, Tentacle Lord, Giant Golem, Mana Ancient. Your bread-and-butter farm.
Item bosses Summoned by using a specific summon item at a specific spot. The summon item has a cooldown. Kill it, loot it, wait, do it again.
Raid bosses The big scripted fights — Gnosis, Alteia and Arcana tiers. Once killed they don't come back on their own. They're revived with the Memory Stone instead.

The Memory Stone

Used to revive a boss that's already been killed, so the lobby can fight it again without waiting for a new game.

Difficulty scales with the group

  • More players means tougher bosses and better rewards — more HP per extra member, but also a higher item drop rate and more experience.
  • Hard mode exists on many bosses: a harder version with a better loot chest, which the game labels as coming from Hard Mode.
  • Practice mode lets you learn Alteia-tier fights safely — the boss deals 75% less damage and takes 300% more, but drops nothing. Use it to learn mechanics without wasting a real attempt.

Every Arcana and Alteia fight is written up phase by phase on the Boss Guides page. Read the one you're about to attempt. These fights are lost to unknown mechanics far more often than to low damage.

Loot, personal loot, and Wish

Who gets the drop, and how to aim at the one you want.

Personal loot

Drops are personal: the game rolls separately for each player, so what you get is yours and nobody can ninja it. Nine other people in the lobby doesn't mean a ninth of the loot.

Drop rates are low by design — a typical equipment drop is well under 1% per kill. Every rate on Boss Drops is the base per-kill chance before Icons, party size and boss modifiers push it up.

Loot chests (Arcana and above)

The top bosses don't scatter items on the ground. They leave a loot chest in the middle of the map, lootable by everyone who was in the arena when the fight started — including people who died during it. The chest also lets you inspect the drops and their recipes.

The Wish system

From Alteia tier upward you can make a Wish before the fight, naming one specific item. Then:

  • The wished item's drop rate doubles.
  • Nothing else can drop for you. All in on one item.
  • Not wishing keeps every item on the table, but at a heavily reduced rate.

So: wish when you're hunting one specific missing piece, don't wish when you're happy with anything. Use the Build Maker to work out which single item you're actually short of before you commit.

Crafting and farming

Almost nothing good drops finished. You build it.

Good items are recipes. A weapon might need a lower-tier weapon, two dropped materials, and another crafted item — which itself has a recipe, which has its own recipe underneath. Trees run several layers deep.

This is why farming is planned rather than random:

  • Pick the item you want in the Item Codex and expand its craft tree. It shows every raw material at the bottom and which boss drops each one.
  • Or build the whole five-slot set in the Build Maker and let it produce one combined shopping list, sorted by boss.
  • Import your character in My Characters and everything you already own is crossed off that list automatically.
  • Many items have alternative recipes — a second way to build the same thing out of different parts. If one material is refusing to drop, check whether there's another path.

Money and currencies

Gold is the small one. The coins are what matter.

CurrencyWhat it's for
GoldOrdinary money from killing things. Consumables, summon items, some NPC purchases.
Prius Silver CoinLowest coin tier. Buys Neptinos items. Traded up at the coin trader in Prius Capital.
Prius Gold CoinMid tier. Buys Gnosis items, plus field boss Icons and the Spirit Beast Icon.
Prius Platinum CoinHigh tier. Buys Alteia items. Also converts into Coins of Effort.
Coin of EffortThe serious currency. Earned slowly from top-tier loot chests and coin conversion.
SoulstonesDropped by Arcana bosses. Spent at the Mage Tower to buy levels 101–110, and used in top recipes.

The three Prius coins map straight onto three grades — Silver buys Neptinos, Gold buys Gnosis, Platinum buys Alteia. That makes coins a way to buy a gear tier outright instead of waiting on drop luck, which is often faster than farming the item you're missing.

The main NPCs to know: the coin trader, the Weird Magician (coin and costume exchange), the Mage Tower, and the stat reset NPC by the Recall Stone — all in Prius Capital. There's also a Hearthstone in Alfon Village and Prius Capital to set your respawn point, which is worth doing on arrival.

Commands worth knowing

Typed into the normal game chat.

-analyze and -record need an item first. Buy the Power Recorder from Jaina, who stands in the top-right corner of the Mage Tower — and the Mage Tower is at the top-centre of Capital Prius. Worth buying early: being able to read a boss's stats and see your own damage is how you work out whether your gear is actually the problem.

Command lists change between versions. If one doesn't work, check the Change Logs or ask on the Discord.

Mistakes almost every new player makesMistakes that cost people items

  • Not saving. Then losing four hours. This is the number one way people quit TWRPG.
  • -save with no filename, then not being able to find the file among a folder of random names.
  • Not saving the replay when a Token or a high-grade Icon finally drops. See Save your replay.
  • Never backing up the CustomMapData folder. One reinstall, every character gone.
  • Dumping stat points into the wrong attribute. Only your main stat really pays.
  • Selling or dropping "junk" materials. Check the Item Codex first — that grey rock is probably in four recipes.
  • Farming without a target. Decide which item you're building, get its material list, then farm the bosses on that list. Aimless killing is very slow.
  • Walking into an Alteia or Arcana boss blind. Read the guide first, or use Practice Mode.
  • Wishing for the wrong item. A Wish blocks every other drop. Be sure it's the piece you actually need.

The short version. Four things: what you wear, how you get it, how to aim your luck, and what the words mean. Two minutes. Switch to another tab above when you want the detail.

Gear

You wear five things. Weapon, Headwear, Armor, Wings, Accessory. That's it.

Weapons are restricted. Each hero can only hold certain types — melee, bow, gun, staff, bag. A staff is useless to a gun hero. The Build Maker only shows you weapons your hero can equip, so use it and you can't get this wrong.

Every item has a grade. The grade is its power tier, and it's also the route you take through the game:

Magic / Rare Deltirama Neptinos Gnosis Alteia Arcana

Each grade is dropped by its own set of bosses. You farm one grade to build the gear that lets you kill the next one. Most top gear also needs level 100.

Recipes

Good items don't drop. You build them. A weapon is a recipe: some dropped materials, plus other crafted items, which have recipes of their own. The trees go several layers deep.

So keep everything. Materials that look like junk are recipe parts. Check anything before you drop it.

Don't farm at random. Pick the item you want, look up what it's made of, then go kill the bosses that drop those parts. That's the whole game loop:

  • Item Codex — any item, its full craft tree, and where each part drops.
  • Build Maker — plan all five slots, get one combined farming list.
  • My Characters — paste your save codes and everything you already own is crossed off that list.

Wishes

Before a late boss (Alteia grade and up) you can make a Wish — you name one specific item you want.

  • That item's drop chance doubles.
  • Nothing else can drop for you. It's all or nothing.
  • Not wishing leaves everything on the table, but at a much lower rate.

Wish when you're missing one exact piece. Don't wish when anything would be an upgrade. Work out which piece you're actually short of in the Build Maker first.

Glossary
